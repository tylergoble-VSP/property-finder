How every number on this page was made

The reader this page is written for is a family making a six-figure decision, not an analyst. Nothing below is a number you have to take on trust.

The score

Each home is read two independent ways against homes that have actually sold nearby, and it only counts as a deal when both agree.

1 · The fitted market. A regression of price on size, bedrooms, bathrooms and home type over recent sales gives every home an expected price and a z-score: how many widths of the market’s own scatter the asking price sits below that expectation. At z ≤ −1 a home is priced outside ordinary variation — statistically underpriced, not merely cheap.

2 · The nearest sales. The closest recent sales of similar size, ranked by distance and nothing else — the appraiser’s method, kept deliberately independent of the regression so that agreement between them means something.

The score starts at 50 and adds points for statistical value, for both methods agreeing, for time on the market and for observed price cuts. Every card above shows that exact arithmetic, line by line, and the lines add to the score. A score trimmed at the top of the scale writes the trim down as a line too.

    Glossary

    Deal score (0–100)
    Negotiating room, not appraised value. 75+ GREAT · 60+ GOOD · 45+ FAIR · below that, OVERPRICED.
    z-score
    How far below (negative) or above (positive) the expected price a home lists, measured in widths of the market’s own scatter. About −1 is the edge of normal.
    Sold-price basis
    Where sale prices are public they are used directly (disclosed). Texas and other non-disclosure states publish none, so the post-sale estimate — revised after closing toward what the home fetched — stands in (proxy), and every figure resting on it says so. This report’s basis is in the footer.
    Confidence
    HIGH needs both a thick comp set and a strong fit. A market that discloses no sale prices never reaches it. LOW means verify by hand before acting.
    Location adjustment
    A size-and-shape model cannot see that one pocket is worth more than another, so nearby sales’ own residuals are smoothed over the map and added back. It moves the yardstick, not the score — without it, every ordinary house off the golf course reads as a bargain.
    Monthly carry
    What holding the home actually costs each month: loan, property tax, insurance, association dues, and the improvement-district assessment. Two homes at one asking price can differ by hundreds a month, and the district bill is usually why.
    Cut so far
    The first asking price this tool ever recorded against today’s — cumulative, so two cuts and a rise net out to one number.
    Flags
    Reasons a low price may be earned rather than mispriced. They never lower a score; they lower how much of it you should believe.
    New construction
    Scored against the builder’s own published plan prices for that size, not against resales. Builder incentives are invisible to a listing feed.
