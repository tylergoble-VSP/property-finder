What the live data changed

Every number in this document was measured against the real SearchApi Zillow feed on 16 August 2026, not assumed. Five findings materially change how the tool should be built. Each is flagged measured where it comes from that sweep and judgement where I am choosing rather than observing.

1 callper listing to learn the agent's name — attribution is detail-tier only
19.0%of listings over $1M are raw land, not homes
51%of luxury listings have exactly 40 photos — the MLS cap
8%field coverage on year_built — the renovation pitch is not buildable
  1. The richest luxury markets in DFW fall just outside the ring. Southlake (28.3 mi), Trophy Club (28.3), Westlake (27.3) and Colleyville (25.3) are all beyond 25 miles. Lindsey's market is therefore the west-side Fort Worth luxury corridor plus the Parker County estate belt — a genuinely different market from the Southlake money everyone chases.
  2. Agent identity costs an API call — but almost nothing else does. The search endpoint returns no agent and no brokerage; agent_name lives only in the detail endpoint's attribution block. Against that, price cuts, Showcase, 3D tours and builder attribution all come back free in the sweep — so the opportunity signals fire on day one and detail calls stay reserved for attribution and renovation mining (§3).
  3. Photo count does not separate good agents from great ones. It saturates: the NTREIS MLS caps listings at 40 photos and 51% of luxury listings sit exactly there. Photo count is a powerful negative screen — it finds under-presented listings — but a weak positive score. This partially contradicts the original hypothesis and is dealt with head-on in §3.
  4. Stalled luxury is the dominant opportunity, not an edge case. Median days-on-market for a clean luxury listing is 73 against 57 for the whole market, and the top quartile sits past 135 days. A quarter of the luxury book is stalled at any moment.
  5. Luxury specialists genuinely exist and are few. 141 distinct agents hold the 209 attributed luxury listings, but the 22% who hold more than one control 47% of the book. One agent holds 15. The power law is real, so ranking works.

Recommendation

The gate

LUXURY = price ≥ $1,000,000
  AND price / sqft ≥ $275
  AND sqft ≥ 2,000 and sqft IS NOT NULL
  AND home_type ∈ {SINGLE_FAMILY, TOWNHOUSE, CONDO}

With a second tier — ESTATE at price ≥ $2,000,000 — used for weighting, not filtering.

Why a combination rather than one measure. The question asks whether to use absolute dollars, a local percentile, or $/sf. Each fails alone in this specific market:

ApproachWhy it fails here
Absolute dollars alone$1M in Parker County buys 6,800 sf of ordinary construction on acreage — land value, not design value. Measured: 15 listings ≥$1M sit below $275/sf, including a $1,890,000 / 9,316 sf home at $203/sf and a $1,399,000 / 6,820 sf home at $205/sf. These are big cheap houses. A designer calling on them is wasting a call.
Local percentile alonePercentiles are computed per submarket, so the top 10% of Ridglea (≈$500K) would qualify while the bottom of Westover Hills would not. It makes the threshold move for reasons that have nothing to do with whether a design budget exists.
$/sf aloneA 1,400 sf cottage in Monticello at $480/sf is $672K. Beautifully finished, no furnishing budget at that transaction size, and the seller is not hiring a designer to prep it.
Combination ✓The absolute floor guarantees a transaction large enough to carry a design fee; the $/sf floor guarantees the money went into the building rather than the dirt; the sqft floor and home-type filter remove land, lots and condos-by-error.

Calibrating the two floors

  • $1,000,000 is the 95.7th percentile of active listings and the 97.7th of closed sales region-wide (Redfin export, 5,804 listings / 3,316 sales, 90 days to 31 May 2026 — externally sourced and independently confirmed). Within the three luxury-corridor ZIPs I sampled directly it reads as only the ~83rd percentile, because those ZIPs are luxury-skewed — which is precisely the argument against a percentile threshold: the same dollar figure is the 83rd percentile or the 96th depending on which ZIPs you count. An absolute floor does not move.
  • The industry-standard floors are lower, and that is a deliberate choice to reject. The Institute for Luxury Home Marketing sets its certified luxury threshold at $680K for Tarrant County and $830K for Parker County; Williams Trew — Fort Worth's leading luxury house — markets its Luxury Portfolio book at $750K+; Realtor.com puts DFW-metro luxury entry (top 5%) at $952K. externally sourced These are the right anchors for a metro-wide product. Lindsey's is not one: her geography is weighted west, into Aledo, Walsh and Weatherford, where the median home is already $565K–$690K. A $750K floor there captures ordinary move-up inventory — the external research says so in as many words ("$750K will over-capture in Aledo/Walsh and Weatherford… if your geography is weighted west, $850K–$1M is the more meaningful line"). $1M is the floor that isolates the design budget in this specific corridor. The brokerage concentration (Compass, Williams Trew, Briggs Freeman = 38% of the ≥$1M book) confirms these are the firms operating above the line.
  • $275/sf is ≈ the 70th percentile of the whole market ($225 median, $293 at p75), and sits comfortably below every Tier 1 submarket median ($360–$458). Measured effect: it removes 15 of 214 clean luxury listings — 7.0%. Six of those are in 76008 and four in 76087, exactly the Parker County land-value trap it is designed to catch. A $300 floor would remove 14.6%, which I judge too aggressive; $250 removes only 4.7% and lets the worst offenders through.
Where I am judging, not measuring

The $275 and 2,000 sf figures are my calibration choices. The distribution they sit in is measured; the decision about where to cut it is not derived from any outcome data, because none exists yet — we have no record of which listings ever produced a design engagement. Both should be treated as v1 settings to be revised once Lindsey has logged 20–30 real conversations.

The $1M floor is a deliberate departure from the sourced industry thresholds ($680K/$750K/$830K), not an endorsement of them. Those are correct for a metro-wide luxury product; I am setting the bar higher because Lindsey's west-weighted geography would otherwise fill the list with $600K–$800K Aledo and Weatherford move-up homes that carry no design budget. If Lindsey later extends to the Southlake/Westlake side (§1), the floor should arguably drop toward $850K there, since those markets clear $1M as routine inventory. The floor is geography-dependent by design.

Texas is a non-disclosure state

Sold listings return no price at all — measured: 0 of 123 sold listings in 76107 carried extracted_price. Any threshold applied to sold inventory must use zestimate as a proxy, which was present on 86% of them. This directly limits use case (b), the just-closed-buyer referral — see §4, signal I.

Identifying a luxury specialist

First: the data architecture this forces

The single most important engineering fact, and it is not optional. The search endpoint returns no agent and no brokerage. Agent identity lives only in the detail endpoint's attribution block, at one API call per listing.

TierCostFields available
sweep 1 call ≈ 41 listings Always present: zpid, address, lat/lon, extracted_price, beds, baths, sqft, lot_sqft, home_type, home_status, status_text, days_on_zillow, listing_sub_type, images[], listing_type, thumbnail
Conditionally present (emitted only on qualifying rows): is_showcase, has_3d_model, open_house, price_change, price_reduction, date_price_changed, builder_name, new_construction_type, tag
detail 1 call per listing attribution.agent_name, attribution.broker_name, attribution.mls_id, photo_count (true count), year_built, description, page_view_count, favorite_count, tour_view_count, price_history[], price_per_square_foot, property_tax_rate, virtual_tour_url, home_insights, brokerage_name, walk_and_transit_scores
history free, accrues daily Repeat presence across sweeps, cumulative price movement, status flips, time-to-pending, book growth or decay per agent

More is free than the brief assumes — use it before spending a call. Four signals that look like they should cost a detail call do not:

  • is_showcase — the strongest presentation signal — is free per listing. Measured: 8 of 268 rows carried the key, every one true.
  • price_change, price_reduction and date_price_changed are free, which means the price-cut opportunity signal fires on the very first sweep — no stored history required. Measured: 96 of 214 clean luxury listings carried a price change, 89 of them cuts. This materially accelerates the programme: Lindsey has a call list on day one rather than in month three.
  • has_3d_model and open_house are free presentation-investment signals, alongside the tag object which carries price_cut, three_d_home, showcase, open_house and days_on_zillow types.
  • builder_name and new_construction_type are free on new-construction rows, making the unfurnished-luxury-spec pitch (signal E) a sweep-tier detection.

Detail calls should therefore be reserved for the three things that genuinely require them: agent attribution, true photo_count, and description + year_built for renovation mining — and only on listings already shortlisted by the free signals.

Conditional fields: absent ≠ false

These fields are emitted only on qualifying rows, so a missing key is ambiguous between "false" and "not reported for this row type". In the live sample is_showcase appeared on new-construction and Benbrook rows but was absent across an entire 76107 resale page. Every instance found was true, which is consistent with "emitted only when true" — but that is not proven, and the two readings are not distinguishable from the data collected. Treat absence as UNKNOWN, never as a negative. The scoring model already reflects this: Showcase only ever adds points and never subtracts. Confirming the semantics is a one-hour validation job (spot-check 20 non-Showcase listings against the detail endpoint) and should be done before the field carries 10 points of weight.

The pipeline, and what it costs

Sweep the ring cheaply → filter to the §2 gate → enrich only survivors → aggregate per agent. Measured inventory suggests roughly 300–400 clean residential luxury listings live in the ring at any moment. Enriching all of them costs 300–400 calls, or 3–4% of the 10,000/month quota. To get the concentration denominator (below) the net should widen to ≥$750K, which measured at ~1.5× the ≥$1M count — call it 500–600 calls/month, still under 6%. Agent attribution is affordable. It is simply not free, and it must be gated.

Identity is fuzzy — plan for it

agent_license would be a perfect join key but is present on only 8 of 209 records (4%). Agents must therefore be keyed on normalised (agent_name, broker_name). The normalisation is real work, not a formality: the live sample contained "Compass RE Texas, LLC" and "Compass RE Texas, LLC." as separate strings, and one agent — Allen Crumley — split into two records across "Williams Trew" and "Williams Trew Real Estate", costing him roughly 15 points of score. Strip trailing punctuation, case-fold, collapse whitespace, and maintain a manual brokerage alias table.

The presentation signal, honestly assessed

The brief calls photo count and Showcase "the designer's actual wedge." The strategic instinct is right; the measurement does not work the way one would hope, and it is worth being precise about why.

SignalBase rateVerdict
photo_count = 40 exactly51%Saturated. 40 is the NTREIS MLS photo limit, not an API truncation — confirmed because one listing returned 70, and search len(images) matched detail photo_count exactly on every one of six sampled listings (40/40, 37/37, 22/22, 70/70). Every strong agent measured sits at 36–40. It cannot separate good from great.
photo_count > 401.4%Rare and meaningful. Exceeding the MLS cap means the agent uploaded directly to Zillow — a deliberate, discretionary presentation spend.
photo_count < 158.9%The real value of this field. A negative screen that finds under-presented luxury listings — Lindsey's pitch surface, not an agent-quality score.
is_showcase3.7%High precision, low recall. Zillow Showcase is a paid product; buying it is direct evidence of presentation budget. But at 3.7% it will only ever flag a handful of agents. Weight it heavily when present; never penalise absence.
virtual_tour_url present93%Discard. Effectively universal in luxury — it discriminates nothing.
tag = three_d_home / has_3d_model7.0%Use this instead. A genuine Matterport-class tour — someone paid a crew to scan the house. Free at sweep tier, and available two ways: the tag object and the has_3d_model flag.

The reframe: photo count identifies bad presentation, not good. The clearest illustration in the live data is an agent at a builder's in-house brokerage carrying three $1.1M listings with a median of 5 photos and 0 page views — production inventory masquerading as luxury. Presentation investment is best detected through the rare signals (Showcase, >40 photos, 3D tour), and presentation failure through the thin-photo screen.

Luxury sits 1.3× the market median and the gap widens with price. Borrowing the market-wide 57-day median would flag most of the luxury book as stalled and the signal would mean nothing. Use band-specific thresholds: stalled = DOM ≥ that band's p75; deeply stalled = DOM ≥ 178 (luxury p90).

Independent corroboration — and a refinement

A separate Redfin-export pull (5,804 active listings, region-wide) found the same ratio from the other direction: $1M+ inventory sits at 100 median active-DOM against 69 market-wide — 1.45×, confirming the gap is real and not a sampling artefact of my eight ZIPs.

The refinement matters for the pitch: the luxury market here is bifurcated, not uniformly slow. Desirable product in the best neighbourhoods sells fast — Redfin's neighbourhood tracker shows sold-DOM of 12 days in Rivercrest, 17 in Mira Vista, 14 in Mistletoe Heights — while weaker product languishes (Park Hill 87 days, Colonial Hills 444). So a stalled listing in a fast neighbourhood is a stronger signal than the raw DOM suggests: the comparable homes around it are moving in two weeks and this one is not. When the pipeline has neighbourhood-level sold-DOM, the stalled test should be relative to the listing's own neighbourhood, not just its price band. externally sourced

05

Ranking to twenty, not three hundred

Two scores, never merged into one. An agent is worth a relationship because of AQS; worth a call this week because of OS. Collapsing them into a single number hides which of the two is driving the rank, and the two demand different opening lines.

Opportunity Score

OS = 4 × (thin-photo luxury listings)
   + 3 × (stalled luxury listings)
   + 2 × (listings with a price cut)
   + 5 × (listings hitting signal H — stalled and thin)
   + 4 × (unfurnished luxury new-construction specs)

Thin-photo outranks stalled because it is the deficiency Lindsey directly fixes; a price cut scores lowest because it is common (41.6%) and therefore weakly diagnostic on its own. weights judged

The call list

  1. Gate on quality. Require AQS ≥ 35. Measured: this yields 25 agents from 141 — almost exactly the target list size. AQS ≥ 45 yields only 6, which is too few; AQS ≥ 25 yields 56, too many.
  2. Rank the survivors by OS descending. Quality qualifies; opportunity orders.
  3. Break ties on proximity to Lindsey's service centre — a 5-mile Montserrat call beats a 24-mile Granbury call at equal score.
  4. Force-include any agent holding a signal-H listing regardless of AQS. Seven listings in the sample carry it, and each is a near-perfect fit even if the agent is otherwise unranked.
  5. Cap at two agents per brokerage office for the first pass. Measured concentration means an uncapped list would be a third Compass and Williams Trew; referral networks overlap, and breadth is worth more than depth in week one.

The single best proxy for "this agent will take the call"

Recommendation

A price cut in the last 21 days on a luxury listing already past its band's median days-on-market.

Detectable entirely at sweep tier from price_change and date_price_changed, with no extra API cost.

The reasoning, and why it beats the alternatives:

  • It is an admission, in writing, with a date. Cutting a price is a public, timestamped statement that the current approach is not working. The agent has already had the difficult conversation with the seller and is actively looking for the next lever. Lindsey is not creating the problem awareness — she is arriving just after it peaked.
  • It is timed. The 21-day window is what separates this from every other signal. DOM and photo count are states — true for months, with no reason to call today. A price cut is an event. It gives a reason for the call to be happening now, which is the difference between a cold call and a timely one.
  • It has already cost real money. Measured median cut: $54,150. Against that, a staging engagement is obviously the cheaper experiment — and the agent, not Lindsey, is the one who has to justify the next cut to the seller.
  • It is common enough to sustain a pipeline. 41.6% of luxury listings carried a cut, 15.4% a material one (≥5%). A signal that fires on 3% of inventory cannot fill a call list.

The runner-up — any stalled luxury listing — is more numerous but strictly weaker, because nothing has changed and there is no reason the call is happening today rather than last month. And the intuitive choice, a high AQS, answers a different question entirely: it predicts whether the agent is worth reaching, not whether they will pick up.

Stated plainly

This recommendation is reasoning, not evidence. The frequencies are measured; the claim that a recent price cut predicts call-answering is an inference about human behaviour, and there is no outcome data in this system to test it against. The correct next step is to make it falsifiable: have Lindsey log which signal triggered each call and whether it converted. After ~40 calls the weights in §3 and §5 stop being judgement and start being fitted. Until then, treat every weight in this document as a hypothesis with a number attached, not a finding.

06

Provenance and known gaps

What was measured

  • Live SearchApi zillow and zillow_property calls, 16 Aug 2026.
  • 268 listings ≥$1M across 8 core ZIPs; 214 after the clean-residential filter.
  • 466 all-price clean residential listings in 76107/76126/76132 for market baselines.
  • 209 successful agent attributions (98% success rate) across 141 distinct agents.
  • Distances: haversine against GNIS/OSM centroids, independently cross-checked.

What was externally corroborated

  • Redfin Data Center trackers + listing export (5,804 active / 3,316 sold, region-wide, 90 days to 31 May 2026); Realtor.com ZIP inventory (Jul 2026); FRED median-list series; ILHM certified luxury thresholds; Williams Trew and Briggs Freeman Sotheby's published segmentation.
  • Independently confirms: the $1M+ DOM premium (1.45× region-wide), the west-side median price levels, the Walsh two-tier structure, and supplies the ILHM/$750K threshold authority the live feed could not.
  • Small-n neighbourhoods (Westover Hills, Montserrat, Rivercrest — 1–7 sales/quarter) have directional $/sf only; treat as ranges. Sold-side DOM by price tier is not published — the band table uses active listings, so the ratio is reliable but the absolute level runs high.

Known gaps — stated rather than papered over

  • No outcome data exists. Every weight is calibrated to the shape of the distribution, not to what actually converts. This is the largest single weakness of the model.
  • Concentration is not yet computable — it needs the ≥$750K enrichment net.
  • Persistence is not yet computable — it needs ~90 days of sweep history.
  • year_built is 8% populated, so the renovation pitch (signal F) is not buildable as specified.
  • agent_license is 4% populated, so agent identity depends on string normalisation and will carry some error.
  • Sold-side prices do not exist in Texas, capping the precision of the buyer-referral use case.
  • Submarket $/sf medians for 76179, 76248, 76049 were not computed — those ZIPs straddle the ring edge and need per-listing radius filtering first.
